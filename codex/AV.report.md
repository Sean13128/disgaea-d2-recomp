**Attributions and accounting.**

- Seven steady pollers are confirmed: six NisGraphics threads in `mfc_poll → ps3_poll_backoff`, plus RSX submit. In hub-sample.txt they spend approximately 97% and 99.9% respectively in the condition-wait path. `runtime/platform/guest_poll.c:169` specifies 100 us, but **10,000 actual wakeups/s/thread is not measured**: timeout duration excludes scheduling, mutex reacquisition, DMA/guest work and oversleep.
- Twelve FIOS threads are confirmed in `sys_lwcond_wait`, predominantly `Sleep(1)` with mutex waits and semaphore broadcasts. A requested 1 ms does not prove exactly 1000/s. “1.3% CPU each” cannot be derived from this wall-time sample: blocked `mutexwait` samples are not CPU execution. Process task accounting instead shows median approximately **227 CPU ms/s in kernel mode** overall.
- `game3.log:2825`, `3440`, `4671`, `5286` support main approximately **2000/s** at LR 0x002FC970, ATRAC approximately **1850/s** at 0x00161438 plus **182/s** at 0x0015F890. The histogram keys only on LR (`runtime/syscalls/sys_timer.c:140`), so ATRAC counts aggregate all three lines; its printed tid=20 is the first thread, not attribution of all calls to that thread. “ms slept” sums requested delays, not elapsed sleeping time. Counts are unlocked estimates; the duplicated/interleaved block at lines 4054–59 demonstrates their limits. Early LR 0x003247EC approximately 4000/s is loading-phase FIOS polling and disappears in the hub.
- **70k + 12k + 4k = 86k, not 71k.** Excluding host audio and other timers, 71k would leave approximately 55k for seven pollers, or approximately **7.9k/thread**, entirely plausible for a nominal 100 us wait. This is an illustrative budget, not a recovered per-thread measurement. Neither sample nor POLLTOP counts the poll timeouts themselves.
- powermetrics “Wakeups (Intr)” counts interrupt-caused thread dispatches, not every condition signal/handoff; “Pkg idle” is a separate subset. Context switches also include semaphore contention, condition signals, global broadcasts and ordinary preemption. Therefore csw, interrupt wakeups and idlew should not be equated or subtracted to produce an exact handoff count. These measurements also come from three separate runs.
- RSX render has **78/3562 = 2.19% wall samples** in `__gettimeofday → _pthread_cond_wait → wait_until_locked → WaitForMultipleObjects` (79 gettimeofday leaves total). This corroborates expensive repeated timed waits, but does not establish “2.3% of a core” or that every wake came from PUT changes.
- SDL dummy's SDLAudioP2 thread is asleep in all 3562 samples; the host cellAudio thread is mostly asleep, and Metal command/completion workqueues have very few samples. None appears to dominate this capture. **CoreAudio was absent** (`game*.log`: `driver=dummy samples=256`), so the user's actual audio configuration remains unmeasured. Audio/mixer event handoffs remain normal contributors even when largely asleep.

**Ranked findings and correctness risks.**

**1 — High: A needs a bounded, role-aware timeout; 2–4 ms everywhere is unsafe.** `guest_poll.c:160–176` registers then checks bytes under the bucket lock. However `guest_poll.h:22–26` has relaxed global **and line** guards; the slow path's seq_cst filters do not repair an early return. Concrete race: waiter registers, reads old bytes; writer commits new bytes but skips notify after observing zero/stale filter; waiter sleeps until timeout. Changing the guard load to acquire alone is not a complete store/load handshake. Keep a finite fallback unless the publication protocol and every writer are changed.

This timeout also covers **ongoing missing notifications**, not just registration: `runtime/ppu/ppu_memory.h:152–162,200–205` inline HLE stores notify only on reserved lines through coherence; an ordinary unreserved write has no poll notification. Direct mapped memcpy writers also exist. A quiet poller at a 4 ms cap can therefore delay work by approximately 4 ms plus scheduling latency, every time it wakes for such a producer.

There is a potential liveness failure beyond “slightly late”: `libs/video/cellGcmSys.c:1695–1715` publishes successive reference values at a **200 us** minimum interval (the adjacent 1 ms comment at 1725 is stale). An equality waiter can miss a transient target after a missed notification and remain stuck once the reference advances past it. This risk exists at baseline under scheduling stalls; a blanket 2–4 ms cap makes it worse. A 4 ms delay also consumes most of the **5.333 ms audio block** budget or misses a nearly-ready 60 Hz flip.

SPU coverage is mostly present: ordinary PUT notifies at `runtime/spu/spu_dma.h:711`; reserved PUT additionally notifies under the lock-line lock at 694/703. Successful PUTLLC/PUTLLUC notify through `spu_channels.c:694,762 → spu_coherency.c:208`; reservation-loss peers also get `spu_ch_wake`. These are distinct coherence/event semantics: preserve them, including self-reservation handling and lock ordering. Longer poll waits must not delay DMA completion without rereading delivered data (`spu_dma.h:198`), or replace reservation events with polling.

Implementation recommendation: exponential **100 → 200 → 400 → 800 → 1000 us initially for idle graphics workers**, retaining 100 us for PPU hotreads/audio-sensitive paths and RSX fallback initially. Keep registration/recheck and caller rereads. Store timeout streak in TLS keyed by EA, size and snapshot (and reset on a new polling sequence/change); caller repeats cannot encode it because mfc repeats saturate at 40 and RSX always passes 40. Grow only on actual timeout with unchanged bytes, not unrelated broadcasts/spurious wakes; don't reset merely because a hashed bucket was signaled. Consider 2 ms only after latency verification, not 4 ms as the first default.

**2 — High: B is the correct FIOS fix, but a naïve condition wrapper can deadlock.** `runtime/ppu/ppu_sysprx.cpp:338–359` currently ignores signals and timeout and returns success after releasing/reacquiring a semaphore. The comment at 319 about one-shot CRT/SPURS being adequately served by a no-op is stale for FIOS and is not evidence that one-shot handshakes can omit synchronization.

Use a host wait queue associated with the real 8-byte guest condition and its queue identity. Enqueue/select under one host lock **before releasing the lwmutex semaphore**, then atomically release that host lock into a blocking wait. Otherwise the signaler acquires the released lwmutex, signals before the waiter parks, and the waiter sleeps forever. A per-waiter selected flag is needed; a shared generation counter or stored semaphore credit alone does not implement one-target `signal_to`, and can accidentally preserve a signal for a future waiter.

Never hold the condition queue lock while reacquiring the guest semaphore: signaler may hold that semaphore and need the queue lock to signal, causing lock inversion. Remove/select timed-out nodes under the same queue lock as signal selection; prevent stale `signal_to` after timeout, cancellation or destruction. `signal` selects one current waiter, `signal_all` selects current waiters, and `signal_to` uses **guest thread ID**, not pthread/Mach ID. Preserve timeout in microseconds (`r4`, zero=infinite), recursive count, and ownership/error semantics on reacquisition; timeout normally returns with the lwmutex reacquired rather than leaving its semaphore available.

Retain the current semaphore-based lwmutex: it intentionally permits cross-thread release (`ppu_sysprx.cpp:135–145,312–315`). Replacing it with a pthread mutex to use pthread_cond_wait directly reinstates the known ownership-handoff deadlock. Conversely, stamping the saved owner unconditionally can resurrect another thread's ownership: validate legitimate waiting ownership and restore the calling guest identity/recursion consistently. Check behavior against [RPCS3's lwcond implementation](https://github.com/RPCS3/rpcs3/blob/master/rpcs3/Emu/Cell/Modules/sys_lwcond_.cpp), especially owner validation, recursive preservation, targeted signals, and timeout/relock behavior. Signal need not always be called by the lwmutex owner.

Existing `libs/system/sysPrxForUser.c:660–777` already has blocking lwcond prior art, but its separate mutex table/host-mutex model is incompatible with these active context handlers (`ppu_sysprx.cpp:982–987`). Merely fixing that library will not fix this capture. D2 uses raw SPU groups, not SPURS (OPERATIONS.md); generic SDK verification must additionally exercise CRT/static-init and cellSpurs startup. One-shot waits are particularly good at exposing a missing signal formerly concealed by unconditional success.

**3 — Medium/high, missed contributor: global multi-wait broadcast storms.** `runtime/platform/win32_compat.c:170–173` broadcasts **s_multi for every changed object** whenever any multi-waiter exists. Each successful FIOS `ReleaseSemaphore` at 337–350 therefore wakes RSX render's `WaitForMultipleObjects(kick,timer)` (`cellGcmSys.c:1839–1840`), even though neither object changed. Render rescans, computes deadlines and sleeps again while contending on the single global `s_lock`; all FIOS semaphore operations use it too. Audio event signals do the same. This is a concrete explanation for extra csw and timed-wait/gettimeofday work that does **not require RSX PUT kicks**; its exact frequency needs counters.

B eliminates much of the trigger load. If it remains material, route notifications only to multi-waiters subscribed to that object (with registration/state checks under s_lock), rather than broadcasting to everyone. Do not simply remove the global broadcast: real kick events, timer rearming and wait-all transitions must still wake interested waiters. As a smaller follow-up, Darwin relative condition waits in `wait_until_locked:253–261` can avoid the realtime conversion, but fixing fan-out has greater value than optimizing repeated useless waits.

RSX submit also duplicates direct PUT kicks: lifted stores already call `ppu_gcm_write_notify` (`ppu_loader.cpp:1509–1532`), while the watcher detects them again (`cellGcmSys.c:1757–1759`). A satisfied label produces repeated kicks until cleared (`1761–1765`). Audit all direct/HLE/DMA PUT and label producers before deleting the watcher; the existing fallback covers genuine gaps. The monitor sometimes waits on a label instead of PUT, so new work via an unnotified PUT may wait for the fallback timeout.

**4 — Medium/high: C should not lower the whole audio/mixer chain to UTILITY.** Blanket USER_INTERACTIVE is real (`sys_ppu_thread.c:114`, `guest_poll.c:267`; RSX copy/submit and `cellAudio.c:741`). NisGraphics SPUs already use DEFAULT (`runtime/syscalls/lv2_register.c:690–691`); synth2 uses interactive. Audio includes more than the synth2 SPU: retain cellAudio, `_cellsurMixerMain`, `synth2_generate`, related mixer workers and ATRAC at current priority initially. Demoting their producer/consumer chain can miss blocks even with synth2 left high; semaphore-based lwmutexes offer no normal owner-based priority-inheritance relationship across guest holders.

Begin C with **FIOS DEFAULT** as a separate experiment, with loading/stream-refill latency checks; UTILITY is a larger change because those workers serve foreground scene loads and ATRAC refills. Keep main, render/submit/copy and audio at current QoS until measured. Put D2 name/role classification in a game-specific policy/override, not hardcoded D2 names in the generic SDK. Apple confirms QoS affects throughput and timer latency, but this does not prove a particular M4 DVFS response: [Mac QoS guidance](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Performance/Conceptual/power_efficiency_guidelines_osx/PrioritizeWorkAtTheTaskLevel.html).

Two omissions matter here. First, `libs/audio/cellAudio.c:605–614` polls the host deadline with repeated **1 ms / 200 us sleeps**, plus SDL room polling (`audio_backend_wait:258`); it wakes more often than its 187.5 Hz block rate, is absent from guest POLLTOP, and will remain after A/B. Second, `libs/video/rsx_metal_backend.m:3767–3770` holds `NSActivityLatencyCritical | NSActivityUserInitiated` for the entire session (Z). This is another plausible energy-policy influence, not a measured cause. Preserve it for the first fixes; any later experiment should remove only LatencyCritical while retaining the App Nap/user-initiated activity, and rerun Z's hitch/background-audio checks.

W fixed device-driven two-block consumption bursts; Z added deadline-debt recovery and a 4.33 ms producer reserve; AP fixed notify-key startup ordering and ATRAC looping/refill. Neither a simple longer audio sleep nor “lower audio QoS” is established safe by dummy-audio profiling. AP's cached-loop defect was not caused by QoS, but delayed FIOS/ATRAC refills can create fresh underruns. Agree with D: leave guest usleep loops unchanged for this pass.

**Recommended order and verification (future implementation; none executed by AV).**

1. **A, narrowly:** cap graphics idle timeout at 1 ms with actual-timeout/change tracking and preserve short waits elsewhere. Extend `test_guest_poll.c` and `test_guest_poll_filter.c`: notified publication must beat the cap; unnotified/registration-race publication must complete within cap plus scheduling tolerance; resets, hash collisions, cross-line writes and cancellation must preserve state. Existing tests prove eventual progress with generous limits, not a 2–4 ms latency budget. Run existing SPU DMA/coherency tests; measure graphics work-start latency and guest flip intervals in hub, hallway, battle and movie/boot fence-heavy paths.
2. **B:** implement/test all four operations together in the active context-handler path. Exercise signal between release/park, signal while holding/not holding lwmutex, signal-before-enqueue with no saved credit, N waiters/signal-one/all/to, recursion, timeout racing signal, destroy/recreate, cancellation and cross-thread lwmutex release. Check actual guest/FIOS signal paths and CRT/SPURS one-shot boot. Do not ship an indefinite wait with no-op/misrouted signals. Confirm idle FIOS threads remain blocked, then load/save, change maps and stream music repeatedly.
3. **Multi-wait fan-out, only if still substantial after B:** count object-change broadcasts versus matching kick/timer wakes. Extend compatibility tests with unrelated semaphore storms (no false progress), real kick wake, timer rearm, auto-reset semantics, wait-all, timeout and close/lifetime behavior. Preserve exact vblank phase; inspect worst-frame intervals, not just average 60 fps.
4. **C, FIOS first:** measure DEFAULT independently; retain the entire audio chain and rendering workers. Under competing CPU load, verify scene transitions/refills and frame/audio deadlines before considering additional demotions. Audio-priority or process-activity changes are last, separate experiments.

For each step use the same binary/ELF version, save copy, hub scene, resolution, focus and FPS settings; no parallel build load. Collect `top -l 16 -s 2 -pid <pid> -stats pid,cpu,csw,idlew,th`; discard first CPU observation and derive csw/idlew rates from cumulative differences divided by timestamp differences. Add low-frequency **per-thread counters** for poll timeout/notify returns, lwcond waits/signals/timeouts and matching versus unrelated render wakes; do not log every wake. Top alone cannot close the attribution budget. Use a short sample after settling (treat its perturbation separately).

Validate both SDL dummy and **real CoreAudio**, nominal 187.5 guest blocks/s, near-zero unintentional BGM silent blocks, no alternating-block pattern, no ATRAC stall/Decode errors and smooth audible output. Include AP's muted start and hub→map30005→hub plus a full music loop, and W/Z's short hitch/5-second stop and background/focus recovery. Use existing W/Z/AP analysis helpers and AUDIO_RATE/ATRAC diagnostics; judge audio pacing from timestamps as well as averages. Keep 60 Hz flip pacing and record tail frame times. After individual changes pass, run final sudo powermetrics over settled idle→same hub→exit and compare CPU power, P-cluster residency, per-core frequency and interrupt/package-idle wakeups; measure temperature separately if confirming the user's 70 C report.
